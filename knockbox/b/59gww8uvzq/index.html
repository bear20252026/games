Hi-Hat

Not a grid. A drummer.

Draw a beat in most sequencers and it comes back dead straight, every hit the same. Knockbox plays yours the way a drummer would: late where it should drag, early where it should push, soft where a hand goes soft. A love letter to J Dilla and the crooked swing he got out of a sampler. Here is what is going on under the pads.

The pocket

Turn the swing dial up and the kick drags behind the beat while the snare jumps ahead of it. That push and pull is the drunk walk. Friction, Limp and Live are three ways of holding it.

A hand on every hit

The one lands hard, the kick between the beats sits back, the hats rise and fall across the bar. Nothing you have to draw: it is how a hand plays, worked out for the beat on your pads.

Ghost notes

Each drum has a Ghost dial. Turn it up for the quiet notes in between, the ones you feel more than hear.

The switch-up

No drummer plays bar two like bar one. Say yes when he asks and every second bar answers the first, with a lead-back into the top. The notes he adds show on the pads with a dashed rim.

Your loop, locked in

Drop in a loop and it is fitted to your tempo, its hits found and chopped. Drag a hit onto the grid and the audio stretches to fit, same pitch. Follow leans the chops on your kick, snare or hats, so the sample swings with the drums.

Sing it, beatbox it

Record a vocal over the beat and Tune puts it in your loop's key. Beatbox into the mic and it lands on the pads as a pattern.

A drummer who listens

He hears your loop and watches your pads, then speaks up: a snare a step off, a kick fighting the bass, a groove worth trying. Try it, keep it or put it back. He suggests. It is your beat.

Take it with you

MIDI, WAV, stems, or the groove on its own to lay over your drums in your DAW. Every nudge and ghost note comes out as it was played.
